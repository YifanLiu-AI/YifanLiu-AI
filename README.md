Failure-stage diagnosis: Trained π0.5 baselines across all 10 tasks using four H100 GPUs and an RTX 4090. Mixer Operating: Traced pressing failures through raw data, normalization, and control; found 195/1,000 demonstrations with action/state values outside the current joint limits. Manipulate Pipette: Used motion planning as the primary controller, with lateral residual corrections from online π0.5 predictions added to the downward plan to address insufficient pressing depth. The initial submission ranked 2nd globally; the final team rank was 17th. Baseline selection: Trained and evaluated Diffusion Policy and X-VLA; selected X-VLA based on baseline success rates and compute requirements. Closed-loop tuning: Built an automated training, closed-loop evaluation, and configuration-adjustment pipeline after observing that lower loss could yield lower success. At 30k training steps and 60 evaluation episodes per setting, augmentation limits of 0, 1, 2, and 3 yielded success rates of 76.67%, 86.67%, 80%, and 78.33%, respectively. Selected one augmentation, improving long-sleeve success on training-set scenarios from 76.67% to 86.67%. Data flywheel: The official dataset provides 25 demonstrations for each of 40 seen garments; limited data hindered generalization. Adding low-quality simulation demonstrations collected by human experts through master-arm teleoperation reduced success. Independently developed a multi-round RFT data flywheel that fed policy rollouts back into training. Under compute constraints, the team generated roughly 100 rollout trajectories, achieving success-rate gains of 20% on the training set and 2% on the test set. The champion&#x27;s post-competition report described similar components using tens of thousands of rollouts. Trajectory filtering: Clustered rollout episodes by completion time and reward to reduce redundant successful trajectories while retaining longer successes with corrective behavior." width="900"></picture>
